01 · Olin Assistive Technology Lab

Braille Learning Device

An open-source, portable device that helps people who lost their sight later in life learn braille on their own.

My roleUI/UX Design & Outreach sub-team lead
DatesFall 2025 – present
Team7-person sub-team, about 20 in the lab
Users12 co-design participants
ToolsOnshape, co-design sessions, interviews, storyboarding

Problem

Our users are people who lost their sight later in life and are learning braille on their own, often without a teacher beside them.

Our first co-designs tested a wide range of prototypes with several user groups. From that we chose to design for new braille learners.

What I did

  • Lead a 7-person sub-team that connects user research to the hardware.
  • Planned and ran co-design sessions with blind educators and braille students: their learning stories, a mock lesson, and a magnetic blank-slate model where they placed the buttons themselves.
  • Designed the full exterior housing in Onshape and worked out space claims with the firmware and hardware sub-teams.
  • Now co-developing the lesson plans the device will teach.

What users changed

User tests showed a gap we could fill for new learners: speech to braille. It is now a non-negotiable feature.

Users also set the 8–12 hour battery target and asked for audio feedback to find a misplaced device. Jumbo braille at 3× standard size makes the dots easier to feel while learning.

Design requirements we set

RequirementTarget
Overall size (L × W × H)≤ 8 × 3 × 1.5 in
Weight with electronics≤ 225 g
Drop survival36 in onto a hard floor
Braille dot durability10,000 finger swipes
Braille cells8–12
Battery life8–12 h

Process

  1. Spring 2026Go broad

    Wide set of prototypes; picked braille learners as the user group.

  2. Spring 2026Narrow

    Learning-tool prototypes; user feedback set the final form.

  3. Fall 2026Validate

    Checking every feature against research and new co-designs.

  4. NextTeach

    Lesson plans and logic handed to firmware.

02 · Formlabs Hackathon

Resin Cure Sensor

A sensor built into a Form 3 build plate that detects when each layer of resin has cured.

RoleBuild plate CAD, sensor integration
DatesSpring 2026
Team4 people
ResultDetected cure of each layer
ToolsOnshape, custom PCB, MATLAB

How it works

A coplanar dielectric sensor, a PCB impedance comb, sits in the build plate. As liquid resin cures into a solid, the impedance across the comb changes, which could let the printer tell when a layer is done instead of relying on a fixed exposure time.

What I did

I modeled the modified build plate in Onshape so it could hold the PCB comb flush with the print surface. We then swept the sensor across different frequencies and printed a test coupon on the modified plate.

03 · Mechanical Prototyping

Origins Kinetic Sculpture

An album cover brought to life: a walking figure, swaying vines, a rising building and spinning flowers, all from one motor.

MechanismsSlider crank, 2 scotch yokes, cam, belt, chain, bevel gears
DatesSpring 2026
Envelope18 × 18 × 8 in
TeamGroup project
FabricationManual mill (Delrin), lathe, plasma cut and bent sheet metal, laser cutting, 3D printing

Build

Four core mechanisms run off a single chain drive: a slider crank for the vines, two scotch yokes for the walking man, a cam for the building, and a belt drive with 1:1 bevel gears to spin the flowers. The build used 3 milled Delrin parts, 1 turned part and 9 plasma-cut parts bent on sheet metal tools.

Challenges

We expected heavy friction in the scotch yokes and slider crank, so we used Delrin and ball bearings and tensioned the belt carefully. Access was tight, so we cut away half of one side panel and installed the flowers last.

What I'd change

Everything moves in sync. Different sprocket sizes would give each element its own rhythm. The chain also started skipping a little near the end of the showcase.

04 · Mechanical Prototyping

Sinking Ship

A mechanical re-enactment of the sinking of the RMS Lusitania: a U-boat fires a torpedo that strikes the ship.

Mechanisms3 belts, 2:1 gearbox, scotch yoke, DC motor
DatesSpring 2026
TeamGroup project
LearnedOnshape variables, manual mill, lathe
FabricationLaser cutter, manual mill, metal lathe, ShopBot CNC, 3D printing

Tensioning

Belt tension wasn't solved in CAD. We re-cut and re-printed several parts and rebuilt the tensioning system for one belt entirely before it ran reliably.

Designing for the mill

The scotch yoke was meant to be our milled part, but the first design couldn't be machined. We redesigned it for the mill and moved a few other parts to the laser cutter to simplify fabrication.

Result

Every mechanism works. Their timing doesn't line up well enough to sell the moment of impact. Syncing the motions is the next fix.

05 · Computational Robotics

Computational Robotics

Three ROS 2 projects on a Neato robot vacuum fitted with a Raspberry Pi: a robot that obeys hand signals, a particle filter, and a wall follower.

PlatformNeato, Raspberry Pi, lidar, camera
DatesFall 2026
SoftwareROS 2, Python, OpenCV, MediaPipe, YOLO
TeamsPairs and a trio
Codeneato_dog · robot_localization · sid-trevor-fsm-p1
Machine vision · team of 3

Neato D.A.W.G.

The Dynamic Autonomous Walker with Gestures obeys three hand signals read with MediaPipe. An open hand makes it "sit" by backing up about 0.8 m. A zero sign makes it spin a full circle using odometry. A peace sign makes it "fetch": it searches for a red marker using a custom-trained YOLO model and drives to it.

neato_dog · MediaPipe · YOLO · ROS 2
Localization · team of 2

Particle Filter

Estimates where the robot is on a map of a campus building by weighting candidate poses against lidar scans. We tested it by replaying recorded bag files in RViz.

robot_localization · lidar · RViz
Control · team of 2

Wall Detect and Follow

The robot drives a star pattern until it bumps something, checks with lidar whether it's a wall, lines its side up with it, then follows it by balancing readings 30° ahead and behind. On the real robot, friction threw off the timed turns. That taught us to trust sensors over open-loop timing.

07 · BOW DataFest 2026

Rural vs. Urban Diabetes Care

Does living farther from the hospital mean a longer stay for diabetic patients? We tested it with Kansas hospital records and census data.

AwardBest Use of Outside Data
DatesSpring 2026
Team5 people
ToolsMATLAB, census data

Method

Filtered the hospital dataset by keyword to diabetic patients, linked records by ID, and classified each patient as rural or urban using census data. We mapped the distribution, calculated each patient's distance to their hospital, and ran a linear regression against length of stay.

Finding

We expected farther patients to stay longer. The regression line came out flat: no significant correlation. Patients clustered within a few miles of the hospital, nearly all stays were under a day, and the long-stay outliers were almost all close to the hospital, possibly because patients far from home don't want to stay long.

Limits we named

Length of stay is a rough stand-in for severity, and we didn't control for age, cost of care, or hospital capacity. Better severity data is the clear next step.

08 · Solo projects

Mechanism and Joinery Studies

Two solo builds under tight material limits: one for gear and cam motion, one for joints and fasteners.

DatesSpring 2026
Constraint18 × 24 in of wood per piece; ≤ 40 g PLA
ToolsOnshape, SolidWorks, Blender, laser cutter, 3D printing

Smoke + Automata

A cam lifts a bird while a crank-and-shaft linkage moves two hands at a changing speed, as if they're trying to catch it. Chipboard and wood prototypes showed I needed an extra gear for the hands to move correctly. The cam turned out too steep, so I added a last-minute attachment to the dowel. Next time I'd test the cam profile earlier.

Imagine a Box

An exploration of joints and fasteners: stub and through mortise and tenon, butt, dado and box joints, plus L brackets, screws, nails and glue. The pentagonal pillars meant box joints at non-90° angles, so I ran chipboard and eucaboard test cuts to dial in tolerances. I also cut and filed down screws that stuck out of the back.

10 · Health wearable

Circulatory Jacket

An interactive jacket that turns the wearer's heart rate and stress into flowing light.

SensorsHeart rate, galvanic skin response
OutputLED strips
ToolsArduino, Rhino

Light cycles from red, for oxygenated blood leaving the heart, to blue, for blood returning to it.

The LEDs flow faster as heart rate rises, and take on a yellow tint as the skin response sensor reads more stress.
